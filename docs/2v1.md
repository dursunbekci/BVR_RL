# Training 2v1

Two blue aircraft, flown by one shared policy, against one red. The code is
`bvr_team.py` (the fight), `bvr_team_vec.py` (the trainer's view of it),
`train_bvr.py --format 2v1` (training), `eval_2v1.py` (comparing checkpoints)
and `team_roles.py` (what happens when red opens on the rear aircraft).

## What the fight models

| | |
|---|---|
| Blue | Each aircraft has its own radar, track, observation and shaping, as the 1v1 agent. Over the datalink each can fight on its wingman's firm track. Kill, loss, timeout and escape rewards go to both. |
| Red's sensors (SIM_REV 22) | One radar track per blue aircraft, from the agent's own radar code run from red's side. Red fires at an aircraft only on a firm track of it, and each missile is guided on red's track of its target. No missile, blue's or red's, has the 3-second support rule (as in 1v1 since SIM_REV 18, and in DCS): one whose shooter loses the track flies on to its last estimate. A low-RCS blue aircraft is detected later (detection range goes as RCS^1/4). |
| Red's opening target | Drawn per episode: the nearer aircraft (half), the farther or a random one (a quarter each), held until red's first launch. |
| Red's tactics | Scripted SHOOTER and ADAPTIVE, with ADAPTIVE's full 1v1 variety (deep defences, long shots, pressing, running, DCS speeds); at the last stage, optionally a frozen 1v1 policy (`--red-policies`). |
| What red knows (`--red-awareness`) | `gci` (default): scripted red steers on the blue aircraft's true positions, as if a controller gave it the picture. `radar`: it steers on its own radar track, coasting when the track drops; before it first detects an aircraft, on that aircraft's start position and velocity, dead-reckoned. A policy-flown red always uses its own radar. |
| Launch delay | Per platform (`LAUNCH_DELAY_MIN/MAX`, plus `LAUNCH_BUG_MIN/MAX` when the track is younger than `LAUNCH_BUG_READY_S`): F-16C-DCSFIT 2.5-4 s for the release, 4.5-10.5 s more on a track held under 25 s (measured in DCS); GENERIC-UCAV-DCS 2-5 s (illustrative). The shot leaves only if the target is still tracked within the fire cone then; the fire option is shut while waiting. Applies to blue and to a policy-flown red; scripted red (the DCS AI's stand-in) fires at once. |
| Starts (SIM_REV 22) | 70% the 1v1 set-ups for the lead (head-on, offset, beam, stern; half swapped), 30% free geometry: any angle off and aspect, half of those at 30-60 km. A faster red (the UCAV case) keeps the starts where it is behind blue and drops only those where it runs. Speeds by Mach 0.75-0.95 within each aircraft's own choices (a UCAV 212-250 m/s), a fifth of the F-16 starts at Mach 1.0-1.3. Wingman: abreast, trail, echelon, a wide split (15-30 km abeam) or a long trail (10-20 km), in a quarter of the starts 3-4 km above or below the lead, in a quarter 10-30 deg off its heading. |
| Start of the fight (SIM_REV 22) | A picture call, as in 1v1 since SIM_REV 21: until an aircraft's radar has a track, its heading choices are offsets from the bearing to red at the start (a policy-flown red: to each blue aircraft). |
| Cost of a lost blue aircraft (`--blue-loss-cost`) | 1.0 by default, as a kill is worth 1.0. Lower it for an attritable UCAV: at 0.5, trading a UCAV for the kill nets +0.5, above a timeout (-0.8). |

## Two manned F-16s against one

Warm start from the newest 1v1 model of the same platform, `models_bvr/dcsfit_v6.zip`
(F-16C-DCSFIT v F-16C-DCSFIT, SIM_REV 20, mirror-averaged policy, in git; the
wingman inputs are part of every observation, zero in 1v1), with it and an
older 1v1 checkpoint as red at the last stage:

```
python train_bvr.py --format 2v1 --platform F-16C-DCSFIT --opponent-platform F-16C-DCSFIT ^
    --resume models_bvr\dcsfit_v6.zip ^
    --red-policies models_bvr\dcsfit_v6.zip models_bvr\dcsfit_v4.zip ^
    --symmetric-policy --max-steps 400 --n-envs <cores - 2> --run-name dcsfit_2v1
```

Use the same `--max-steps` in `eval_2v1.py`: the policy reads the elapsed time.

## Two UCAVs against one manned F-16

```
python train_bvr.py --format 2v1 --platform GENERIC-UCAV-DCS --opponent-platform F-16C-DCSFIT ^
    --red-awareness radar --blue-loss-cost 0.5 ^
    --red-policies models_bvr\dcsfit_v6.zip models_bvr\dcsfit_v4.zip ^
    --symmetric-policy --max-steps 400 --n-envs <cores - 2> --run-name ucav_2v1
```

- **The UCAV is illustrative** (`library/user/platform/GENERIC-UCAV-DCS.json`,
  airframe `GENERIC-UCAV-LW`): a loyal-wingman-class jet of 4.55 t, one
  18.7 kN engine (thrust/weight 0.42, a little above the XQ-58A Valkyrie's
  0.33 from public figures), Mach 0.82-0.83, 7 g, the F-16C-DCSFIT's g lag
  (1.0 s building up, 1.9 s coming off), RCS 0.1 m², 2 AIM-120C-DCS (the
  F-16's missile, so both sides use one missile model), a smaller radar
  (70 km against a 1.2 m² target) and a 2-5 s launch delay for an operator's
  consent. The builtin `GENERIC-UCAV` airframe (26 kN, 0.58, no g lag) is a
  high-end, fighter-like UCAV. Replace the values with the concept you are
  studying; RCS and launch delay are worth sweeping (e.g. 0.01, 0.1, 0.5 m²).
- **Not modelled, and it favours the UCAV:** the F-16's RWR does not detect
  the UCAVs' radars, which in reality could be heard beyond their own
  detection range.
- **`--red-awareness radar` is what makes the low RCS count.** With `gci` red
  still knows where the UCAVs are and only shoots later. Train against both if
  you are not sure which the manned side would have; evaluate against both.
- **Red is faster** (460 against 250 m/s): stern starts are skipped (blue
  could never catch a running red), and a red that runs ends in a timeout or
  escape. Expect more timeouts than in the F-16 2v1.
- **Warm start:** a 1v1 F-16 policy's speed choices mean different speeds on
  the UCAV, so `--resume` from it only helps with the tactics; starting from
  scratch is the clean choice. A 1v1 UCAV-against-F-16 model is not a useful
  stepping stone: alone, the UCAV is outmatched.
- **Red policies** are 1v1 F-16 models; the run notes when one was trained on
  another F-16 platform (e.g. dcs_v8 on F-16C-DCS).

## From the GUI

`python bvr_gui.py`, TRAIN tab: set FORMAT to 2 v 1, LEAD FLIES and WINGMAN
FLIES (e.g. GENERIC-UCAV-DCS, or "as lead"), OPPONENT FLIES (F-16C-DCSFIT),
EPISODE 400 s. The 2 V 1 OPTIONS block under the wingman's platform sets RED
AWARENESS, BLUE LOSS COST, RED POLICIES (Ctrl/Cmd-click several) and RED
POLICY SHARE; SYMMETRIC POLICY is further down. They are the command-line
options above.

## Comparing checkpoints

```
python eval_2v1.py models_bvr\ucav_2v1.zip models_bvr\archive\*ucav*.zip ^
    --red-policies models_bvr\dcsfit_v6.zip --red-awareness radar --max-steps 400 --episodes 100
```

Same starts, opening rules and radar noise in every cell. Report the score,
clean kills, blue aircraft lost per episode and the exchange ratio, against
both red awarenesses for a UCAV team.

## Not modelled

- Chaff, notching and ground clutter (on either side), and red's RWR
  detecting the UCAVs' radars: an emitting UCAV is as hidden as a silent one.
- Off-board sensors for blue (AWACS or a manned controller's datalink): each
  UCAV sees only through its own radar and its wingman's.
- Fuel and endurance.
- In DCS, 2v1 needs one DCS client per policy-flown aircraft (multiplayer); the
  DCS link is 1v1 only.
